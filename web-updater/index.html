Step 1 · Flash the firmware

Installs the firmware itself. Only needed when the firmware version changes — if Step 2 shows your firmware is already up to date, you can skip straight to Step 2.

Download firmware.uf2 Download as .zip

On Windows, antivirus or SmartScreen sometimes blocks or quarantines a raw .uf2 download. If that happens, grab the .zip instead — unzip it, then drag the firmware.uf2 inside onto the RPI-RP2 drive.

Then put the Pico into BOOTSEL mode

Already running TS-Pico firmware and connected in Step 2? Skip the buttons — just click Enter BOOTSEL mode below. Otherwise, use the manual steps:

  1. Remove the TS-Pico from the expansion board.
  2. Plug the USB cable into your computer.
  3. Plug the other end (Micro-USB) into the Raspberry Pi Pico.
  4. Press and hold the Pico Reset button.
  5. While still holding Reset, press and hold the BOOTSEL button on the Pico (the little white button next to the Micro-USB connector).
  6. Release the Reset button.
  7. Release the BOOTSEL button.
  8. A drive named RPI-RP2 will appear on your computer. Find it in your file manager — don’t open or click into it.
  9. Drag the downloaded firmware.uf2 onto the RPI-RP2 drive.
  10. The Pico reboots into the new firmware. Leave it connected by USB and continue to Step 2.
Already connected in Step 2? This reboots the Pico straight into the RPI-RP2 drive — no buttons needed.

Step 3 · Copy the SD card files testers

For software testers only. The TS-Pico reads TAP programs and tpi:help text from a FAT-formatted SD card at runtime, so to exercise the firmware end-to-end on your own hardware you need a card with these files on it. Download the bundle and copy its contents to the root of the card you plug into the TS-Pico. Production users can skip this — a ready-made SD card image will be provided separately.

Download SD card files (.zip)

Then copy them onto the card

  1. Format an SD card as FAT32 (or reuse one already prepared for the TS-Pico).
  2. Unzip the downloaded sdcard.zip.
  3. Copy everything inside the unzipped folder to the root of the SD card, preserving the subfolders:
    • TAP/ → /TAP/ (test programs)
    • help/ → /help/ (tpi:help text)
  4. Eject the card, insert it into the TS-Pico, and reboot. You should see INFO: SD Card initialized and mounted OK.
